                        The Calculus Engine f(t) and f'(t)
                    

                    

                        

                            
Cumulative Growth Function

                            

                                $$f(t) = \frac{L}{1 + C \cdot e^{-k t}}$$
                            

                            

                                Where $L$ is the mature ceiling weight, $k$ is the acceleration constant, and $C = (L - W_0) / W_0$ is the constant scaling the initial birth weight ($W_0 = 15$ kg) at $t = 0$.
                            

                        

                        

                            
First-Order Growth Derivative

                            

                                $$f'(t) = \frac{L \cdot C \cdot k \cdot e^{-k t}}{(1 + C \cdot e^{-k t})^2}$$
                            

                            

                                Represents the instantaneous rate of change (weight gain rate) in kilograms per month. Multiplying $f'(t)$ by $\approx 32.89$ yields the industry-standard **Average Daily Gain (ADG) in grams/day**.

                        Bioscience Context
                    

                    

                        Biological growth follows a sigmoidal pattern because animals have finite mature dimensions due to cellular constraints. The derivative $f'(t)$ tracks dynamic metabolic demands. The peak of $f'(t)$ (inflection point) occurs precisely when the body's rate of tissue formation is at maximum. At this stage, feed formulation efficiency is highly optimized.
                    

                


                
                

                    

                        
                        Malaysian Farm Management
                    

                    

                        For local breeds like Kedah-Kelantan (KK) beef cattle, defining pasture strategy depends on knowing the inflection age. Breeders in locations like Kelantan and Terengganu can use this mathematical modeling to decide when to move calves from costly feedlot diets to simple maintenance pasture, thereby preserving profit margins.
